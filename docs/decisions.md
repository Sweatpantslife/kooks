# Current decisions and open questions

Reconciled October 9, 2026 against the source. See [the developer handoff](handoff.md) for evidence, tests, and acceptance criteria. Personal discovery notes are not part of the public repository.

## Selected and implemented

- Household cooking is the product scope: recipe capture, editing, portions, meals, shopping, equipment, guided cooking, and cooking notes.
- Capacitor is selected for both iOS and Android. Earlier proposals treating platform choice as undecided are historical.
- Node.js and SQLite implement the local MCP backend and persistent browser app. They share validated domain actions and a cookbook.
- The phone app is the same client as the browser's, packaged with Capacitor, and works on the household's kitchen: it asks for an emailed link and waits while the member confirms it on any device, then keeps a device token, because an emailed link opens in the browser and a passkey belongs to the kitchen's web address. (Until October 31, 2026 it typed a six-digit emailed code; sign-in is now links and passkeys only.) The previous phone app's separate cookbook, data model and backup format are retired; the recipes written in it come into the kitchen once, when the member asks. Without a connection the phone shows its copy of the kitchen and keeps the changes that cannot overwrite anyone when they arrive late: grocery ticks, groceries added by hand, leftovers eaten, cooking notes and recipe edits. A recipe edit is met part by part with the kitchen's version when it goes; where someone else changed the same part meanwhile, the member sees both and chooses, and nothing is sent until they do. Photos the phone has opened are kept too, up to 50 MB, the ones opened longest ago going first. Each waits on the phone with a request id fixed when it was made and goes in order when the kitchen answers, so it arrives once; a change the kitchen will not take by then waits with the reason until the member tries again or discards it. Cooking progress and timers stay online: they happen at the stove, within the kitchen's reach, and a timer's deadline is set when the kitchen hears of it (decided October 31, 2026; `docs/native-app.md`).
- All eight household additions are implemented in the browser/MCP layer: pantry suggestions, leftovers/batching, cooking memory/variants, effort filters, taste profiles, shared tasks/timer ownership, cost estimates, and reviewed photo imports. Older deferrals of these additions are superseded.
- Backup/restore and reversible action history exist in the backend. Prototype-era statements saying no backups/history exist do not describe the present code.
- Logic both clients need lives in `shared/` as dependency-free modules; image bytes live outside record documents and history (database schema 2).
- Imported recipe text is data. Preserve source values, unknown quantities, independent shopping contributions, and cooking snapshots. Retries and undo must not overwrite later changes.
- Recipes carry four facets for search and filtering: a course and diet labels from the fixed lists in `shared/taxonomy.js`, a free-text cuisine and free tags. The app filters on them, the MCP server exposes `recipe_taxonomy` so an agent reuses the household's names, and the previous phone app's single label becomes a tag when its recipes come in. A filter never matches an unrecorded facet, and diet labels are household declarations, not allergen checks.
- People sign in with passkeys, with a one-time emailed link as the fallback and as the first proof of an address. Since October 31, 2026 they have accounts: an account belongs to any number of households, each household's cookbook is its own SQLite file (the first one is the server's database), and households grow by emailed invitation, which proves the invited address. `KOOKS_OWNER_EMAIL` seeds the first person; the old `KOOKS_HOUSEHOLD_EMAILS` list seeds the first household once. Sign-up is invite-only unless `KOOKS_OPEN_SIGNUP` is on. Sessions slide to 180 days from the last day of use and can be ended per device; tokens are not rotated, since the phone's token must survive offline work and the devices list covers a lost device. Passkeys are verified with Node's own crypto in `web/webauthn.js` and links are sent by the small SMTP client in `web/mail.js`; neither adds a dependency. Loopback-only use stays sign-in free.
- A member may say which person at the table they are (October 31, 2026). The link belongs to the membership and is kept in the sign-in tables, not in the cookbook: a cookbook and its backups hold no sign-in addresses, and a member who leaves or is removed takes only the link, leaving a plain person. Every change records who made it, by an opaque account id with the person linked at the time, or as an MCP agent's; history names members to one another by the person they were at the table when they made the change (later links never rename it), never by email address, and says "A member" for someone who has not said which person they are.
- The browser app was redesigned from scratch in October 2026 and replaced the previous one: a Preact client in `client/`, built by Vite and served at `/`, that talks to the server only through `/api/state` and the validated tools. Three tabs, Week, Recipes (recipes, meals, ideas, techniques and books) and Groceries (to buy, by aisle, and at home), with Household (people, prices and budget, look and units, sign-in, backup) behind the household's initials. Cooking is a full-screen mode with a Now cooking bar elsewhere. The previous app's addresses redirect. `docs/ia-ux.md` is the authority. The phone app is the same client.
- The look is "Market": warm white, tomato for actions and herb for what is done or at home, Bricolage Grotesque titles and the system sans for text, with a device-following dark theme that Household can fix per browser. Every color is a token in `client/src/styles/tokens.css`.
- Costs moved from each recipe to the week: Household › Prices and budget and one line on the Week count what the planned food comes to. Shared cooking jobs with owners live in cooking mode beside the timers, and a recipe lists every time it was cooked under "Cooked before", with Add a note for cooking done without the stove.
- Recipe links are stored addresses. The app names YouTube, Vimeo, Facebook, Instagram and TikTok videos from `shared/links.js` and opens every link in a new tab, or the phone's browser. The server fetches a page only to add a recipe **From a link**: one public page, read for its schema.org Recipe data into a draft to check, under the guard described in SECURITY.md. Pages without that data are pasted instead; the server does not scrape page text or fetch images.
- Techniques, the inspiration board and the ebook shelf are browser/MCP records (`mcp/library*.js`). Uploaded photos and PDF/EPUB files are immutable assets in SQLite, checked by signature, served only to the household and never read or summarized by the server; portable backups include them.
- The built-in assistant, **Ask Kooks**, uses a Claude API key that whoever hosts the kitchen sets on the server (`KOOKS_ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`); the key never reaches the app, and members do not sign in to a model provider. Without a key there is no assistant, and the MCP server stays the way to bring an agent of one's own. Consumer accounts and subscriptions are not used for API access.
- The assistant reads the kitchen and proposes; it never changes anything itself. Each proposal (a plan, groceries, shopping for a week or a dish, a recipe draft) is described by the app from its own records and made only when a member taps it, through the usual tools, so history and Undo apply. SECURITY.md lists what it reads and its limits.
